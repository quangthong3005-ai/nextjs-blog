A **compressor** reduces the difference between the loudest and the quietest parts of a signal. It makes a performance feel more even and more present in a mix, which is why it is one of the most used effects in music production.

## The main controls

- **Threshold**: the level above which the compressor starts working.
- **Ratio**: how strongly it reduces the signal above the threshold. A ratio of 4:1 means that 4 dB over the threshold becomes 1 dB over.
- **Attack**: how quickly the compressor reacts after the signal crosses the threshold.
- **Release**: how quickly it lets go once the signal falls back below the threshold.
- **Makeup gain**: raises the overall level to make up for the volume that compression removed.

## How it works inside

A simple compressor has two parts:

1. A **level detector** that follows how loud the input is.
2. A **gain computer** that turns that level into a gain reduction, then smooths it with the attack and release times.

The output is the input multiplied by that smoothed gain. The art is in choosing the settings so the effect sounds natural.

## Try it on keys

Piano and electric piano have sharp attacks and long tails. A gentle ratio with a slower attack keeps the touch of the keys while evening out the sustain.
